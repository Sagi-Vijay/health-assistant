Common Cold:
Symptoms include runny nose, sore throat, cough, congestion, slight body aches, or a mild headache. Sneezing and low-grade fever are also common.
Treatment involves getting plenty of rest and drinking fluids. Over-the-counter medicines may help ease symptoms. Antibiotics do not work against cold viruses.

Influenza (Flu):
Symptoms come on suddenly and may include fever, chills, cough, sore throat, runny or stuffy nose, muscle or body aches, headaches, and fatigue (tiredness).
Antiviral drugs can treat flu illness. They can make illness milder and shorten the time you are sick. They may also prevent serious flu complications.

Migraine:
A migraine can cause severe throbbing pain or a pulsing sensation, usually on one side of the head. It's often accompanied by nausea, vomiting, and extreme sensitivity to light and sound.
Treatment includes pain-relieving medications and preventive medications. Lifestyle changes like stress management and regular sleep can also help.

Hypertension (High Blood Pressure):
High blood pressure often has no signs or symptoms, so it's frequently called the "silent killer." Some people may have headaches, shortness of breath, or nosebleeds, but these signs aren't specific and usually don't occur until high blood pressure has reached a severe or life-threatening stage.
Treatment involves lifestyle changes (diet, exercise) and medications like diuretics, ACE inhibitors, or beta-blockers.

Diabetes Type 2:
Symptoms include increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintended weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing sores, and frequent infections.
Management includes healthy eating, regular exercise, weight loss, diabetes medication or insulin therapy, and blood sugar monitoring.
